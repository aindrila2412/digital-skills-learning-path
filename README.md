# Digital Skills Learning Path

Live app: <https://aindrila2412.github.io/digital-skills-learning-path/>

This is a personal portfolio project: an eight-week beginner course on digital, cloud and AI skills, plus a little
app to go with it. I made it to practise designing a programme end to end. It's a design on paper, not a record of a
course that happened. Nobody has taken it, and it isn't linked to any employer, university or training provider.

The app works offline after your first visit. In it you can tick off each week's outcomes, try practice quizzes, rate
your own confidence, browse a free reading list, and see a few cited statistics about why digital skills matter. It
relates to UN SDG 4 (quality education).

Everything you do is saved only on your own device (in the browser's local storage). There's no account and nothing is
sent anywhere. You can export your progress as a file if you want to keep it.

## Installing it on your phone

On Android (Chrome), open the link, then tap Install app on the About tab, or use the browser menu and choose
"Install app" / "Add to Home screen".

On iPhone or iPad, open it in Safari, tap Share, then "Add to Home Screen". iOS doesn't offer an install prompt, so
the About tab lists these steps. Safari can clear storage for sites you haven't opened in a while, so export your
progress now and then.

## Where the numbers and links come from

The context figures in the app come from ITU (Facts and Figures 2025), TRAI (broadband, end of August 2026), MoSPI
(Comprehensive Modular Survey: Telecom 2025), NFHS-6 (2023-24), ASER 2024, and Indian government replies on
digital-literacy schemes. Each one is shown with its source link, its date and its limits, and I checked them on
3 October 2026. Some of these are self-reported or cover only part of the population (ASER is rural 14 to 16 year
olds), and the app says so. I haven't estimated any year a source hasn't published.

The reading list is a set of free material made by other people (Microsoft Learn, CS50, freeCodeCamp, Khan Academy, GitHub Skills and others) and each
item links back to its owner. The quiz questions and course text are my own writing.

The app has no learner data in it. Nothing here reports real learners.
